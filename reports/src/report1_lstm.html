Why the ladder matters here

The data come from a physical rig: a PVP1 ventilator pushing air through a proportional inspiratory valve into an IngMar QuickLung, a bellows lung with selectable resistance R ∈ {5, 20, 50} cmH₂O·s/L and compliance C ∈ {10, 20, 50} mL/cmH₂O. Each breath is 80 samples at about 33 ms; only the inspiratory phase (valve-out closed, 25 to 32 samples) is scored.

The catch is that most breaths were driven by a feedback controller. A PI controller reads the pressure sensor and sets the valve opening from the error to a target. Information flows both ways, so the valve command is partly a consequence of the pressure, not only its cause.

Three variants, defined by what they may read

To predict pressure at step k, each variant gets a different window of the valve command. Everything else (R, C, time, step size, and on Track A the measured starting pressure) is identical.

  • Plant-causal sees valve commands strictly before step k. This is the information a physical simulator has: valve opening drives flow, flow fills the lung, and pressure follows. It is the fair comparison for the physics model in Report 4.
  • Filter-causal also sees u_in[k]. Still legitimate in real time, since the command is issued at k, but it lets the model read the controller's reaction to the pressure it just measured. This is a monitor, not a simulator.
  • Bidirectional sees the whole breath, including the expiratory phase. It cannot run live. Top Kaggle entries used this setting.

Model and training

Network
2-layer LSTM, 256 hidden units (bidirectional: 256 per direction), linear head; pressure = 8 + 10 · head(h)
Inputs per step
u_in/100, u_out, one-hot R (3), one-hot C (3), Δt/35 ms, t/3 s; Track A adds (p[0] − 6)/3 as a constant channel
Plant shift
plant-causal inputs are delayed one step, so step k never contains u_in[k]
Loss
MAE on scored steps only (inspiration; Track A also drops step 0, which is an input)
Optimiser
AdamW, lr 2×10⁻³, weight decay 10⁻², cosine schedule with 5% warm-up, gradient clip 1.0
Budget
30 epochs, batch 512, early stopping on validation (patience 5), best checkpoint kept
Split
54,324 train / 6,036 validation / 15,090 holdout breaths, stratified by (R, C); distributions of u_in, pressure, p[0], inspiration length and PI fraction match across splits (KS p ≥ 0.32)
Hardware
Apple M1 CPU, 4 threads (faster than the M1 GPU for this sequence length)

What the ladder shows

1

The plant forgets its starting pressure within about five steps

For the plant-causal model, knowing p[0] cuts the error by 0.27 cmH₂O at step 1, but the benefit is under 0.1 by step 6 (0.2 s) and about 0.03 after that. Physically this is what a dissipative system under feedback control should do: the controller pulls pressure towards its target regardless of where the breath began, so the initial condition decays faster than the open-loop RC time would suggest.

2

Seeing the current command helps only once the pressure level is anchored

On Track B, adding u_in[k] changes nothing (0.899 → 0.897). On Track A it is worth 0.09 (0.833 → 0.745), and the benefit of p[0] lasts the whole breath instead of fading. Our reading: the controller computes u_in[k] from the pressure it measured at k, so the current command is an indirect pressure gauge. That gauge reads relative to an unknown target and unknown gains; the measured starting pressure calibrates it. This is reading the controller, not modelling the lung.

An interpretation, not a proof. Non-PI breaths gain too (0.804 → 0.727), which fits because the non-PI breaths are also feedback controllers, just learned ones.

3

Bidirectional wins, using information a simulator cannot have

Without any pressure input, the bidirectional model reaches 0.667, better than every causal model with p[0]. Two signatures say where the gain comes from.

  • The start of the breath. Its step-0 bias is half that of the causal Track B models (+0.15 vs +0.30 cmH₂O), and on non-PI breaths its error over the first five steps is 0.37 against 0.69. Our earlier probe found that expiratory u_in predicts p[0] to 0.28 cmH₂O, because breaths were recorded in batches and the previous breath sets the starting pressure. The bidirectional model reads the expiratory phase, so it can identify the batch.
  • High targets. At PI target kt = 35 it beats even filter-causal Track A, 0.85 against 1.07. Future valve commands are the controller's reactions to future pressure, so they reveal where the pressure went.
4

Where the errors live: high resistance, high pressure, and whole-breath offsets

Every variant finds the same cells hard. R = 50 is worst (filter-causal Track A: 0.96 at C = 10) and R = 5, C = 50 is easiest (0.33). Error rises with pressure up to about 40 cmH₂O. In aggregate the bias is near zero, but individual breaths can sit off by a constant: in the 99th-percentile example above all five models overshoot the whole inspiration by about 3 cmH₂O. That pattern suggests a hidden per-breath factor, such as which of the ten ventilators recorded it and how its valve was calibrated. A physics model can carry that as one gain estimated online from early samples.

5

None of these models has finished learning

Every run's best validation epoch was its last one, and the curves are still falling when the cosine schedule reaches zero. The 30-epoch budget is the same for every rung, so comparisons along the ladder are fair, but the absolute numbers are not the LSTM's ceiling. Strong Kaggle LSTMs reached roughly 0.15 to 0.2 with engineered features, hundreds of epochs and ensembling.

What it costs

Streaming is about 95 µs per step, well inside the 33 ms sampling interval, so every causal variant could run live. The bidirectional model cannot stream at all: each output needs the whole breath.

What the physics model has to beat

The fair comparison for a plant simulator is the plant-causal row: 0.833 with p[0] and 0.899 without, from about 800,000 parameters and 128 MFLOP per breath. The proposed targets for the physics model are under 1% of those parameters (about 8,000), at least ten times cheaper inference, and CPU training in minutes. The filter-causal row, 0.745, is the bar for the monitor variant if the simulator alone falls short.

The ladder also hands the physics model two concrete jobs. Track B needs a sensible initial pressure without batch leakage. The level offsets in Finding 4 point to a per-breath valve gain that should be estimated online rather than learned as a constant.

Limits of this baseline

  • One seed per run; differences under about 0.02 should not be read as real.
  • Undertrained by design (Finding 5). A longer schedule would lower every number.
  • The split is random over breaths, so breaths from one recording batch can sit in both train and holdout. The Kaggle test set has the same property; it flatters models that identify batches.
  • Bidirectional Track A has not been run yet.
